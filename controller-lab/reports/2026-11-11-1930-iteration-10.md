## Tradeoff and limitations
- This adds one round-trip per *changed* gamepad state, potentially increasing latency at high polling rates. Unchanged frames remain coalesced and renewed by acknowledged heartbeats.
- A two-second acknowledgement timeout is a safety bound, not a measured latency target. Need benchmark end-to-end at 60/120/240 Hz; a future design should use pipelined sequence-number acknowledgements, bounded in-flight frames, and explicit epoch/slot ownership to avoid synchronous round-trip overhead.
- Keyboard/mouse remain on their existing paths; no driver/hardware validation performed.
- CI for this iteration must be inspected before declaring it passed. No local Windows hardware access.

## Technical basis
- Python `subprocess.Popen.stdin.flush()` confirms buffered bytes are handed to the pipe, not that a remote process executed a command. See https://docs.python.org/3/library/subprocess.html and https://docs.python.org/3/library/io.html .
- Existing C# bridge state handler validates slot and acknowledges only after submission.
